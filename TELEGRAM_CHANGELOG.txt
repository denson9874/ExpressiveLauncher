<b>Expressive Launcher 5.1.9 QA (Build 82) — Don't Recycle What's Still on Screen</b>

5.1.9 fixes a Recents crash introduced in 5.1.8 (81) where Overview cards could throw <code>Canvas: trying to use a recycled bitmap</code> after Android 17 delivered an updated task snapshot.

───────────────────────

<b>✨ What's New &amp; Improved</b>

<b>1. 🛠️ Fix Recents recycled-bitmap crash (5.1.8 regression)</b>
• <b>Before</b>: in 5.1.8 (81), <code>RecentsModel.onTaskSnapshotChanged()</code> stopped unconditionally reporting snapshots as consumed, while <code>TaskVisualsChangedDelegateImpl</code> (and <code>RecentsView</code> on the legacy path) still returned <code>null</code> after handing the incoming <code>ThumbnailData</code> to <code>TasksRepository</code>. Whenever Android 17 delivered an updated snapshot for a visible Recents card that was not currently in the 3-slot <code>TaskThumbnailCache</code> (for example after LRU eviction, memory trim, or <code>lastActiveTime</code> invalidation), <code>TaskStackChangeListeners</code> immediately recycled the live <code>Bitmap</code> right before <code>TaskThumbnailView</code>'s <code>FixedSizeImageView</code> drew it—crashing the launcher with <code>Canvas: trying to use a recycled bitmap</code>.
• <b>Now</b>: <code>TaskVisualsChangeListener</code>, <code>TaskVisualsChangedDelegateImpl</code>, <code>TasksRepository</code>, and <code>RecentsView</code> accurately report <code>true</code> whenever they retain a task snapshot, so live Recents card bitmaps are never prematurely recycled, while genuinely unused snapshots are still freed immediately.

<b>2. 🛡️ Defense-in-depth guards for Recents thumbnails</b>
• <b>Before</b>: <code>TaskUiStateMapper</code>, <code>TaskThumbnailView</code>, <code>TaskThumbnailCache</code>, and <code>TaskThumbnailViewDeprecated</code> only checked for <code>null</code> bitmaps without checking <code>isRecycled</code>, and <code>TaskThumbnailView.resetViews()</code> cleared its <code>ImageView</code> with <code>setImageBitmap(null)</code> (which still leaves an empty <code>BitmapDrawable</code>).
• <b>Now</b>: all four layers guard against recycled bitmaps (falling back cleanly to the task's background color or a cache miss), and <code>TaskThumbnailView.resetViews()</code> clears its drawable with <code>setImageDrawable(null)</code>.
